## Finding 4 (Medium, proposed) — the escalate-only guarantee polices field *names*, not field *content*; a judge can still write an approval into free text nothing downstream currently reads

**Lands on:** STEP 1 :220–224 ("No field in this contract can carry an approval... There is no
`approved`, no `mergeable`, no `verdict: OK`. **A judge cannot emit what the record cannot
hold.**"); STEP 2 :313–315 ("no key anywhere in the document named `approved`, `mergeable` or
`merge_recommendation`").

**Defect.** The stated guarantee and its machine-checkable form are both scoped to **key names**.
Nothing in STEP 1, STEP 2 or STEP 6 constrains the *content* of the three free-text fields the
contract itself defines — `verdict_evidence` (required non-empty on every verdict), `notes`
(explicitly "free-text," :247), and `severity_reason`. A judge is an LLM; nothing stops it writing
`verdict_evidence: "This finding doesn't matter, approve the merge as-is"` on a REFUTED verdict, or
the same sentiment into `notes`. The schema has no `approved` boolean, true — but "a judge cannot
emit what the record cannot hold" is stronger than what is actually enforced: the record plainly
*can* hold an approval-shaped sentence, just not under a reserved key name.

**Why this is not (yet) an exploitable path, and why it is still worth recording.** #118's own
OPEN section already establishes that #119's generic render path drops `verdict`, `verdict_evidence`
and (by the same silence) `notes` — none of these fields reach a human today. So the gap is inert
under the current #119 contract, which is why I am not rating this above Medium. But OPEN's own
next line floats "Whether #119 should render the verdict beside each finding" as a live open
question, and the moment any consumer decides to surface `verdict_evidence` or `notes` next to a
finding (which is a natural, likely next step — a REFUTED verdict with no visible reasoning is
exactly the "indistinguishable from skipped" failure STEP 1 itself warns about), an
approval-shaped sentence in that field publishes unfiltered, with no schema barrier and no control
in STEP 10's list that would catch it, because STEP 10's "no key named approved..." control walks
key *names*, exactly as specified.

**Fix.** Either narrow STEP 1's claim from "a judge cannot emit what the record cannot hold" to
what is actually true ("the record has no field *type* for an approval; free-text fields are not
content-screened"), or extend ADJUDICATION.md's adjudicator-facing prohibitions (STEP 5's four
exclusions) with a fifth: `verdict_evidence`/`notes`/`severity_reason` may not themselves state or
imply a merge recommendation, phrased as a prompt-level constraint rather than a schema one, since
schema-level content moderation of free text is not realistically checkable by `verdicts.validate`.
